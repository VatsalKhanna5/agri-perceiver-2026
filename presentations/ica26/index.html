Why not just ask a big VLM?

T2

Lesions are tiny. Tokens are expensive.

Pustules, water-soaking and early chlorosis vanish when an image is resized. Keeping them needs more visual tokens, and attention grows with their square.

{ }

Chat models write prose.

Decision-support systems consume a fixed schema, not paragraphs. Prompting gets you valid JSON. It does not get you the right content.

7–8B

Billions of weights. One GPU.

Fully fine-tuning a generalist is out of reach for most agricultural labs, and it risks forgetting what the model already knows.

Structure is not the differentiatorevery model emits valid JSON ≥ 99.7% of the time; the gap is entirely in what the JSON says.

Generalists see fungus. They miss the rest.

On bacterial and viral leaves every baseline scores F1 ≤ 0.025. AgriPerceiver reaches 0.56 and 0.53, the only model usable on all six classes.

Where it still gets it wrong.

29%

Yellow looks like yellow.

Viral → deficiency. Diffuse chlorosis looks the same whether a virus or a missing nutrient caused it. Unknown → deficiency is the largest single cell: 169 leaves.

21–23%

Early lesions look fungal.

Bacterial and pest → fungal. At 384 px per tile, early lesions and fungal necrosis overlap.

These are confusions of biology, not of format: clear targets for augmentation.

Confident.
A little too confident.

In the 0.7–0.8 bin, the biggest source of error, the model says 0.80 and is right 0.57 of the time. That gap is inherited: the training targets carry the teacher's own confidence.
